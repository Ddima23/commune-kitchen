# Finnish food safety for a small kitchen

## Authorities
- Mainland Finland: Ruokavirasto (Finnish Food Authority) plus municipal food control.
- Åland (e.g. Brändö): ÅMHM (Ålands miljö- och hälsoskyddsmyndighet) runs food control under Åland's own law. Check with ÅMHM for registration/approval of the premises.

## Self-monitoring (omavalvonta / egenkontroll)
Every food business must have a written self-monitoring plan covering:
- storage temperatures and shelf life,
- allergen handling and labelling,
- traceability (where the food came from),
- cleaning and pest control,
- corrective actions when something goes wrong.
Keep it simple and dated. An inspector (Oiva on the mainland, ÅMHM in Åland) may ask to see it.

## Temperature rules (confirm against current guidance)
- Cold storage: at or below +6 °C; keep the cold chain unbroken.
- Fresh fish: on ice, about 0 to +2 °C.
- Frozen: at or below −18 °C.
- Hot holding: at or above +60 °C.
- Cook / reheat through: at least +75 °C core (or an equivalent time–temperature combination).
- Cool cooked food quickly, in shallow portions, from +60 °C down to +6 °C.
Log fridge and freezer temperatures daily; note and fix any breach.

## Allergens
Label all 14 EU allergens (milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, gluten, soy, sesame, celery, mustard, lupin, molluscs, sulphites). In a communal kitchen, list allergens per dish and collect diners' allergies in advance.

## Inspection readiness
Oiva grades premises (excellent / good / corrected / poor). Inspectors check premises hygiene, self-monitoring records, temperature management, allergen information, and staff training. Keep the records tidy and dated.

## Daily checklist
- [ ] Fridge at or below +6 °C, freezer at or below −18 °C (logged)
- [ ] Hot food at or above +60 °C / reheated to at least +75 °C
- [ ] Allergen list per dish available
- [ ] Use-first for short-dated items (see receipt-checking)
- [ ] Cleaning and waste done, dated
